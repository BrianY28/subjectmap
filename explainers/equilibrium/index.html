Equilibrium is a balance
of rates.

A reversible reaction keeps happening in both directions. At equilibrium, the two rates match and the mixture stops changing overall.

The model and its limits

For the ideal one-to-one conversion A ⇌ B, Q = aB/aA = [B]/[A]. At equilibrium Q = K, so the B fraction is K/(1 + K).

At fixed temperature and pressure, the reaction Gibbs energy is ΔᵣG = RT ln(Q/K), with R = 8.314462618 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹. Its sign indicates the favored net direction. It does not measure an activation barrier.

The animation solves xB(t) = xB,eq + (xB,0 − xB,eq)e−t. We choose forward and reverse rate constants with ratio K and sum 1 in arbitrary units. At equilibrium both rates remain nonzero. Activities and stoichiometric powers are needed for more general reactions.
