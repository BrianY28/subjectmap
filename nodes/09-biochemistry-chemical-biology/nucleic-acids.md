# Nucleic acids

DNA and RNA are nucleotide polymers whose sequence and structure support information storage, recognition and catalysis. Base pairing is central to copying and folding, but interactions extend beyond ideal double helices.

## Concepts

- **Nucleotide chemistry**: A nucleotide contains a base, sugar and phosphate, and phosphodiester links give a chain its directional backbone.
- **Complementary pairing**: Hydrogen bonding and base stacking help stabilize paired structures, with stability dependent on sequence and environment.
- **Replication and transcription**: Polymerases use templates to build complementary strands, with fidelity and error correction affecting information transfer.
- **Functional RNA**: RNA can fold into binding and catalytic structures, so its roles extend beyond carrying messages from DNA.
