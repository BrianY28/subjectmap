# Titration and gravimetry

Classical quantitative methods relate a known reaction stoichiometry to a measured volume or mass. Reliable results depend on reaction completeness, selectivity and a well-defined measurement endpoint.

## Concepts

- **Equivalence and endpoint**: The stoichiometric equivalence point is a chemical condition, while an indicator or instrument reports an endpoint that may differ from it.
- **Titration curves**: Changes in pH or another measured property reveal reaction progress and help choose a suitable endpoint method.
- **Gravimetric analysis**: The measured mass of a compound with known composition can determine the amount of an analyte.
- **Interference and purity**: Co-precipitation, incomplete reaction and competing species can bias the result even when weighing and volume measurements are precise.
