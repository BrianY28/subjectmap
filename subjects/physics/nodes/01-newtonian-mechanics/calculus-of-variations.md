# Calculus of variations

Euler's 1744 treatise turned extremum problems for curves into differential equations, later streamlined by Lagrange into the Euler-Lagrange equation d/dt(∂L/∂q̇) − ∂L/∂q = 0. The method seeks curves making an integral δ∫L dt stationary rather than optimizing a function.

## Concepts

- **Brachistochrone problem**: Johann Bernoulli's 1696 challenge—the fastest descent curve—is the cycloid and launched the variational method.
- **Euler-Lagrange equation**: Setting the first variation of an action integral to zero yields the stationarity condition that later becomes the equation of motion.
- **Geodesics**: Shortest or extremal paths on curved surfaces are found by the same variational machinery, linking mechanics to geometry.
- **Principle of least action**: Maupertuis (1744) and Euler (1744) proposed that motion makes an action stationary.
