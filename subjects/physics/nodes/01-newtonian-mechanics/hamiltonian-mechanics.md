# Hamiltonian mechanics

Hamilton's 1833 reformulation replaces second-order equations with first-order ones, q̇ = ∂H/∂p and ṗ = −∂H/∂q, for H = Σp q̇ − L. The flow becomes a geometry on phase space, leading to canonical transformations and the Hamilton-Jacobi equation.

## Concepts

- **Phase space**: Positions and momenta form a 2n-dimensional space whose points specify complete states and whose flow preserves volume (Liouville).
- **Hamilton's equations**: The pair q̇ = ∂H/∂p, ṗ = −∂H/∂q gives symmetric first-order dynamics generated by the Hamiltonian function.
- **Poisson brackets**: Introduced by Poisson in 1809, {f, g} encodes time evolution via ḟ = {f, H} and identifies conserved quantities.
- **Hamilton-Jacobi equation**: A canonical transformation makes H depend only on new momenta, reducing the dynamics to a single partial differential equation for the action.
