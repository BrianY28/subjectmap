# Rigid-body dynamics

Euler's equations I₁ω̇₁ + (I₃ − I₂)ω₂ω₃ = M₁ (and cyclic permutations) describe rotation of a rigid body about its principal axes. D'Alembert's principle (1743) supplied a general way to handle constraints in extended bodies.

## Concepts

- **Euler's rotation equations**: Torque equals the rate of change of angular momentum in the body frame, producing coupled equations for the angular velocities.
- **Moment of inertia tensor**: A symmetric tensor relating angular velocity to angular momentum, diagonalized by choosing principal axes.
- **D'Alembert's principle**: Inertial forces plus applied forces do no virtual work, converting constrained dynamics into statics-like balance equations.
- **Intermediate axis instability**: Rotation about the axis of intermediate moment of inertia is unstable, now familiar as the tennis-racket or Dzhanibekov effect.
