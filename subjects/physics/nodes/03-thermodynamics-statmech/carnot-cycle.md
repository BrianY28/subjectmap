# Carnot cycle

Carnot analyzed an idealized reversible heat engine within caloric theory and showed that its maximum efficiency depends only on the temperatures of the two reservoirs, not on the working substance. The modern formula η = 1 - T_C/T_H came later, after Kelvin's absolute temperature scale (1848) and Clausius's work in the 1850s.

## Concepts

- **Carnot efficiency**: Carnot showed the maximum efficiency depends only on the two reservoir temperatures; its modern form η = 1 - T_C/T_H follows from the absolute temperature scale and the second law.
- **Reversible cycle legs**: The cycle alternates isothermal expansion and compression with adiabatic expansion and compression.
- **Carnot theorem**: No engine operating between the same two reservoirs can exceed the efficiency of a reversible Carnot engine.
- **Reversibility and maximum work**: Irreversible processes reduce work output, motivating the later concept of entropy.
