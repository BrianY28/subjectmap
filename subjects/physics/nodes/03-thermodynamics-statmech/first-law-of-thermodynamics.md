# First law of thermodynamics

Heat and mechanical work are equivalent forms of energy, and the internal energy of a system is a state function: ΔU = Q - W, with work done by the system. Joule's paddle-wheel experiments provided quantitative support.

## Concepts

- **Joule's paddle-wheel experiment**: Mechanical work churned water and raised its temperature, establishing the mechanical equivalent of heat in the 1840s.
- **Mechanical equivalent of heat**: Joule estimated about 4.2 J per calorie, linking work and heat quantitatively.
- **Internal energy as a state function**: The change ΔU depends only on initial and final states, not on the path.
- **Energy conservation**: The first law expresses conservation of energy for thermodynamic systems, including heat and work.
