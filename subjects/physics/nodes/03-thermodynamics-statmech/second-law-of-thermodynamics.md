# Second law of thermodynamics

Clausius and Kelvin stated the impossibility of certain spontaneous heat-flow and engine processes. Clausius later introduced entropy in 1865, yielding ΔS ≥ ∫ δQ/T, with equality for reversible processes.

## Concepts

- **Clausius statement**: Heat cannot flow spontaneously from a colder to a hotter body without external work.
- **Kelvin-Planck statement**: No cyclic engine can convert heat from a single reservoir entirely into work.
- **Entropy**: Clausius introduced entropy as a state function whose change measures reversible heat transfer divided by temperature.
- **Clausius inequality**: For any cyclic process ∮ δQ/T ≤ 0, with equality only when reversible.
