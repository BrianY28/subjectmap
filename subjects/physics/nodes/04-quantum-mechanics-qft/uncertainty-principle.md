# Uncertainty principle

Heisenberg's 1927 paper gave an order-of-magnitude estimate; Earle Kennard proved the exact bound Δx Δp ≥ ħ/2 that same year, and Robertson (1929) generalized it to arbitrary noncommuting observables.

## Concepts

- **Position-momentum uncertainty**: Δx Δp ≥ ħ/2, proved by Kennard in 1927, limits simultaneous knowledge of position and momentum.
- **Energy-time uncertainty**: ΔE Δt ≳ ħ/2 constrains lifetime and energy spread, though its interpretation is subtle.
- **Complementarity**: Bohr argued wave and particle descriptions are complementary, not simultaneously applicable.
- **Measurement disturbance**: Measurement inevitably disturbs conjugate variables in the Copenhagen view.
