# Schwarzschild solution

The first exact vacuum solution of Einstein's equations describes a static spherically symmetric mass and has a coordinate singularity at r_s = 2GM/c²; its one-way horizon character was only clarified by Finkelstein in 1958. Its line element is ds² = −(1 − r_s/r)c²dt² + (1 − r_s/r)⁻¹dr² + r²(dθ² + sin²θ dφ²).

## Concepts

- **Schwarzschild radius**: The horizon radius is r_s = 2GM/c², proportional to the mass.
- **Event horizon**: Finkelstein (1958) first showed that r = 2GM/c² is a one-way null surface, not a mere coordinate singularity.
- **Birkhoff's theorem**: The spherically symmetric vacuum solution is unique and static, even during spherical collapse.
- **Black hole**: A mass compressed within its Schwarzschild radius forms a region cut off from outside observers.
