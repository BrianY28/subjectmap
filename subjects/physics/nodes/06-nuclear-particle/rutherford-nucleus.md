# Rutherford nucleus

Rutherford interpreted Geiger-Marsden alpha scattering as deflection by a tiny, massive, positively charged nucleus, with most alpha particles passing through and a few scattering at large angles. The Coulomb scattering probability scales approximately as 1/sin⁴(θ/2).

## Concepts

- **Gold-foil scattering**: Geiger and Marsden observed large-angle alpha deflections from thin metal foils in 1909.
- **Rutherford scattering formula**: The Coulomb scattering cross-section explains the observed angular distribution and the failure of Thomson's model.
- **Tiny nuclear size**: The nuclear radius is about 10⁻¹⁵ m, some 10⁴–10⁵ times smaller than an atomic radius.
- **Planetary atom model**: Electrons orbit a compact nucleus, though classical stability problems motivated quantum atomic theory.
