# Atomic clock

Essen and Parry built the first practical caesium-beam atomic clock at the National Physical Laboratory, locking an oscillator to the Cs-133 hyperfine transition. The 1967 SI second was defined as 9,192,631,770 cycles of that transition.

## Concepts

- **Caesium hyperfine transition**: The Cs-133 ground-state hyperfine splitting provides a stable, reproducible frequency standard.
- **Ramsey separated fields**: Two separated microwave interactions produce a narrow interference fringe used to lock the clock.
- **SI second definition**: The SI second is defined by ν = ΔE/h = 9,192,631,770 Hz for the Cs-133 hyperfine transition.
- **Optical clocks**: Optical lattice and trapped-ion clocks, enabled by the optical frequency combs of Hall and Hänsch (Nobel 2005), now reach fractional uncertainties near 10⁻¹⁸, and a redefinition of the SI second is being prepared.
