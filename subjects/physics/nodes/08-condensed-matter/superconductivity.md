# Superconductivity

Onnes discovered zero resistance in mercury below 4.2 K in 1911, and the Meissner–Ochsenfeld effect (1933) showed flux expulsion, captured by the London equation ∇²B = B/λ_L².

## Concepts

- **Zero resistance**: Below a critical temperature, a superconductor carries current without measurable resistive dissipation.
- **Meissner effect**: A superconductor expels magnetic flux from its interior, distinguishing it from a perfect conductor.
- **London equations**: Phenomenological electrodynamics relates supercurrent to the vector potential and predicts a penetration depth λ_L.
- **Type-II vortices**: Abrikosov (1957) predicted quantized magnetic flux vortices in type-II superconductors above the lower critical field.
