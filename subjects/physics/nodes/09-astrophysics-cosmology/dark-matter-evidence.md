# Dark matter evidence

Zwicky (1933) found that galaxies in the Coma cluster move too fast for its visible mass, and Rubin and Ford (1970s) measured flat rotation curves in spiral galaxies. Both imply large amounts of non-luminous matter, with v² = GM(r)/r requiring M(r) ∝ r at large radii.

## Concepts

- **Coma cluster mass discrepancy**: Zwicky inferred far more cluster mass than visible light suggested from galaxy velocity dispersions.
- **Flat rotation curves**: Orbital speeds stay roughly constant at large galactic radii, implying dark matter halos.
- **Mass-to-light ratios**: Galaxies and clusters show M/L far above values expected from stars alone.
- **Bullet Cluster**: In 2006 gravitational lensing showed the mass concentrated apart from the hot shocked gas in the colliding cluster 1E 0657-56, strong direct evidence for dark matter.
