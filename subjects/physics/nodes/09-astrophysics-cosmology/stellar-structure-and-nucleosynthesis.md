# Stellar structure and nucleosynthesis

Eddington's 1926 models treated stars as gas spheres in hydrostatic equilibrium, with energy carried outward by radiation, and Bethe identified the fusion reactions that power them in 1939. The 1957 B²FH paper explained how stars synthesize elements up to iron and, via neutron capture, beyond it.

## Concepts

- **Hydrostatic equilibrium**: Gas pressure gradients balance gravity in a stable star, dP/dr = -Gm(r)ρ/r².
- **Eddington luminosity**: Radiation pressure sets a maximum stable luminosity, L_Edd = 4πGMm_p c/σ_T.
- **Hydrogen fusion chains**: The pp chain and CNO cycle convert hydrogen to helium and power main-sequence stars.
- **B²FH stellar nucleosynthesis**: The 1957 B²FH paper built elements up to iron by fusion, with heavier nuclei made by slow neutron capture (s-process) in stars and rapid neutron capture (r-process) in explosive events; the 2017 kilonova GW170817 confirmed r-process production in neutron-star mergers.
