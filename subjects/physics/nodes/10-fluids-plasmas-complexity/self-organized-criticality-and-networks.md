# Self-organized criticality and networks

Slowly driven dissipative systems can organize themselves into a critical state without external tuning, yielding power-law avalanche statistics P(s) ~ s^(−τ). Network science then showed that topology, from small-world to scale-free structure, controls collective behaviour.

## Concepts

- **Sandpile model**: Bak, Tang and Wiesenfeld (1987) showed a cellular-automaton sandpile drives itself to a critical slope with power-law avalanche sizes.
- **1/f noise and power laws**: Critical states generate scale-free fluctuations, linking earthquakes, forest fires and neuronal avalanches to a common statistical signature.
- **Small-world networks**: Watts and Strogatz (1998) found that a few random rewires sharply reduce path length while preserving high clustering.
- **Scale-free networks**: Barabási and Albert (1999) showed preferential attachment yields P(k) ~ k^(−3), so a few hubs dominate robustness and spreading.
