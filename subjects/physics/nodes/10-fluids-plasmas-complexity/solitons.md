# Solitons

A soliton is a localized wave that survives collisions unchanged because nonlinear steepening exactly balances dispersion. Zabusky and Kruskal coined the name in 1965 while numerically studying the Korteweg–de Vries equation u_t + u u_x + δ² u_xxx = 0.

## Concepts

- **Wave of translation**: John Scott Russell observed in 1834 a solitary water wave that traveled for miles without changing shape.
- **Korteweg-de Vries equation**: Korteweg and de Vries (1895) derived u_t + u u_x + δ²u_xxx = 0, balancing nonlinearity against dispersion.
- **Inverse scattering transform**: Gardner, Greene, Kruskal and Miura (1967) solved KdV by mapping it to a linear scattering problem, revealing infinitely many conservation laws.
- **Optical solitons**: Hasegawa and Tappert (1973) predicted nonlinear Schrödinger solitons in optical fibers, now exploited in long-haul communications.
