# Turbulence

Above a critical Reynolds number flow becomes chaotic and energy cascades from large eddies to small ones where viscosity dissipates it. Kolmogorov's 1941 theory predicts the inertial-range spectrum E(k) ∝ ε^(2/3) k^(−5/3).

## Concepts

- **Reynolds number**: Reynolds' 1883 pipe experiments showed laminar flow gives way to turbulence near Re = ρUL/μ ≈ 2000.
- **Kolmogorov −5/3 spectrum**: Kolmogorov's similarity hypotheses (1941) give E(k) ∝ k^(−5/3) and the exact four-fifths law for third-order structure functions.
- **Boundary-layer theory**: Prandtl (1904) showed viscous effects confine to thin layers near walls, reconciling ideal-flow theory with observed drag and separation.
- **Closure problem**: Averaging the equations generates unknown velocity correlations, so RANS and large-eddy models remain partly empirical — an open modeling question.
