# Beyond the Standard Model

Known: the Standard Model gauge group SU(3)×SU(2)×U(1) fits collider data, and the 2012 Higgs discovery completed its particle content; it still cannot explain neutrino masses, dark matter, baryon asymmetry, the hierarchy or gravity. Unknown: which extension is realized and at what scale; the LHC has found no SUSY or extra dimensions, Fermilab's final 2025 muon g-2 result agrees with lattice-based theory so the anomaly has largely gone, R(K) and R(K*) returned to Standard Model values in 2022 while R(D)/R(D*) still show ~3σ tension, and searches continue at HL-LHC, Belle II, LHCb, EDM and dark-sector experiments.

## Concepts

- **Higgs boson discovery**: ATLAS and CMS found a ~125 GeV scalar in 2012; its couplings match the Standard Model, while the hierarchy problem remains open.
- **LHC null results**: No supersymmetric particles or extra dimensions have appeared; gluino and squark limits reach the multi-TeV scale.
- **Flavor anomalies**: LHCb's R(K) and R(K*) moved back to Standard Model values in December 2022, but R(D)/R(D*) still show a tension of about 3σ.
- **Muon g-2 resolution**: Fermilab published its final result in June 2025 at 127 ppb precision; the 2025 Theory Initiative lattice-QCD prediction agrees, so the anomaly has largely gone, leaving a disagreement between lattice and data-driven estimates of hadronic vacuum polarization.
