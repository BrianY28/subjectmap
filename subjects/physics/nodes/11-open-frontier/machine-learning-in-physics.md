# Machine learning in physics

Known: machine learning is standard in collider event selection, gravitational-wave denoising, survey astronomy, materials screening and neural quantum states, and physics-informed networks solve PDEs; the variational principle E[ψ]=⟨ψ|H|ψ⟩/⟨ψ|ψ⟩ ≥ E0 underlies neural wavefunctions, and the 2024 Nobel Prize in Physics went to John Hopfield and Geoffrey Hinton for foundational work on artificial neural networks. Unknown: whether ML can discover new fundamental laws or deliver calibrated uncertainty and interpretability; no AI-discovered fundamental law exists as of 2026, with Carleo–Troyer neural quantum states (2017) a landmark physics-specific result.

## Concepts

- **Neural quantum states**: Carleo and Troyer (2017) represented many-body wavefunctions with restricted Boltzmann machines, launching ML-based quantum simulation.
- **ML for colliders**: Deep networks tag jets and search for anomalies at the LHC, but no new particle has been found this way.
- **Physics-informed networks**: Raissi et al. (2019) embedded PDE residuals in loss functions, enabling data-driven solutions of physical systems.
- **AI in astronomy**: Survey classifiers and generative models handle billions of galaxies and transients; interpretability and selection biases remain open.
