# Quantum computing and simulation

Known: qubits support high-fidelity gates and small surface-code logical qubits now suppress errors as distance grows, with Google's 2024 Willow result operating below threshold; the 2025 Nobel Prize (Clarke, Devoret, Martinis) honoured macroscopic quantum effects in electrical circuits, the basis of superconducting qubits. Unknown: a large fault-tolerant machine with verified practical advantage; Google's 2025 Quantum Echoes out-of-time-order-correlator experiment claims a verifiable speed-up but practical advantage is debated, and superconducting, trapped-ion, neutral-atom and photonic platforms all face error-rate, scaling and verification challenges.

## Concepts

- **Quantum advantage**: Google's 2025 Quantum Echoes out-of-time-order-correlator experiment claims a verifiable speed-up; practical advantage remains debated.
- **Surface-code threshold**: Below the threshold p < p_th, logical error falls exponentially with code distance; Willow demonstrated this in 2024 but with small logical qubits.
- **Neutral-atom arrays**: Harvard/QuEra ran 48 logical qubits in 2023 and Caltech trapped about 6,100 atoms in one array in 2025, but gate fidelity at scale remains the challenge.
- **Quantum simulation**: Feynman's 1982 proposal underlies analog and digital simulation of materials and chemistry; a clear beyond-classical useful result is still open.
