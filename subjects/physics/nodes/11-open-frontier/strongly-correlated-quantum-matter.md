# Strongly correlated quantum matter

Known: strong electron correlations produce Mott insulators, unconventional superconductivity, fractional quantum anomalous Hall states and quantum spin liquids, often described by Hubbard-type models H = −tΣ c†c + UΣ n↑n↓. Unknown: the cuprate pairing mechanism and pseudogap, whether the Hubbard model itself superconducts, strange-metal transport and how to design higher-temperature superconductors; ARPES, STM, neutron scattering, cold atoms and moiré systems are the main probes, with fractional quantum anomalous Hall states in twisted MoTe₂ and rhombohedral graphene (2023–2024) a recent decisive development.

## Concepts

- **Cuprate pairing puzzle**: Whether cuprates are captured by Hubbard or t–J models, what causes the pseudogap, and what mediates pairing remain unresolved despite decades of ARPES, STM and neutron data.
- **Magic-angle graphene**: Twisted bilayer graphene at the magic angle (2018) shows correlated insulators and superconductivity, a tunable flat-band laboratory.
- **Strange metal**: Cuprates and heavy fermions show resistivity linear in T down to low temperature, violating Fermi-liquid expectations; its origin is open.
- **Fractional quantum anomalous Hall**: In 2023–2024 fractional Hall states appeared without an applied magnetic field in twisted MoTe₂ and rhombohedral graphene, creating a new platform for anyons and topological order.
